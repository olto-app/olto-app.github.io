why olto

Simple on the surface, because it knows you underneath.

It notices when you move

Rides, runs and walks are recorded in the background with just your iPhone. Forgot to start one? olto finds it afterwards and asks you to confirm.

One workout, one truth

When several devices record the same session a little differently, olto checks each against the others and keeps one honest version.

How much, today

A recommended range every morning, and a safe ceiling: the most you can take on before recovery would need more than a few days.

It learns from you

How a session felt, how your body feels, how your shoes fit: your own words count as much as the numbers, and everything feeds one picture of you.

the app

Five pages, one question each.

oltolet's go

Where you start a workout, or let one start itself. It shows how much you've done today and how much is left in the tank.

journalwhat did I do?

Your training diary. Every workout lands here to keep, correct or add to: how it felt, what you wore, a note. Old sessions can be improved later.

formwhere am I going?

Whether you're getting fitter, holding steady or overdoing it, and a plan toward your goal that fits the time you really have.

youwhere am I?

What your body can do right now: fitness, heart, sleep and recovery, each with how sure olto is about it.

gearwhat do I use?

Your bikes, shoes and devices: which one you used when, how far they've gone, how they perform and fit, and when they need care.

And a map of everywhere you've been, with your regular routes, your best times on them and new turns worth trying.

what you tell it

Your feelings are data too.

A journal, not just a log

After a session, say how hard it felt, how your legs and body feel, how you slept, anything that stood out. olto learns what "hard" means for you and weighs tomorrow accordingly.

Gear that learns

olto recognises which shoes or bike you used from where, how fast and how hard you went, and tags the workout for you to confirm. Add how they fit, the size, how they feel after a hundred kilometres: it tracks wear and how each piece performs over time, and tells you when something needs a service or is close to retirement.

Numbers from sensors and words from you end up in the same place. The more you tell it, the more its recommendations sound like you.

your devices

Works with what you have.

olto reads Apple Health, so anything that writes to it counts: an Apple Watch, but also Garmin, Polar, Oura, Whoop, a smart scale or any app that saves workouts, sleep or heart rate there. For the full detail of each workout, two more doors are open.

Apple Health

  • Built into every iPhone, nothing to set up.
  • Your phone's own motion, steps and GPS, plus whatever your watch, ring or apps save there.
  • Sleep, heart rate variability and resting heart rate come from here and set your daily range.
  • Many watch apps save only a summary of each workout: time, distance, average heart rate.
Enough for: most people, from day one.

intervals.icu

  • A free training platform that receives the original file from Garmin, Suunto, Coros, Wahoo, Polar Flow, Zwift and more.
  • Second-by-second heart rate, power, cadence, speed, altitude and laps.
  • Works both ways: sessions olto plans can travel on to your watch.
Worth it for: a non-Apple watch, a bike computer or a power meter.

Polar, direct

  • Polar sensors connect to olto over Bluetooth, no account needed.
  • The most precise heart data there is, live while you record.
  • Sensors that store their own workouts hand them to olto directly.
Worth it for: accurate heart rate on anything, strength and indoor included.

They combine. When the same session arrives through more than one door, olto merges it into one.

set up intervals.icu

Ten minutes, once.

intervals.icu has a lot of options. You only need these.

  1. Create a free accountat intervals.icu, in any browser.
  2. Connect your watch's own appIn Settings, under connections, link Garmin Connect, Suunto, Coros, Wahoo, Polar Flow or whichever you use. Connect it directly: workouts that only arrive through Strava can't be passed on to other apps.
  3. Let planned workouts reach your watch (optional)In that same connection, switch on uploading planned workouts.
  4. Copy two thingsFrom the developer settings: your athlete ID (it looks like i123456) and your API key. Keep the key private.
  5. Paste them into oltoSettings, then sources, then intervals.icu. New workouts then arrive by themselves and wait in your journal.
polar

Two kinds of Polar device, two paths.

Sensors: straight to olto

The H10 chest strap, the Verity Sense and the Loop talk to olto directly. Record with the phone, or record on the device and leave the phone at home: olto downloads the session the next time they meet, and shows sessions the Loop recorded on its own for you to import.

Set up: wake the device, then in olto open gear, then connected devices, and pick it once.

Watches: through Polar Flow

Polar watches send their workouts to the Polar Flow app. From there, link Polar Flow to intervals.icu (steps above) and olto receives the full recording. Polar Flow can also write a summary to Apple Health.

Set up: the intervals.icu steps, choosing Polar Flow as the connection.
how it thinks

Science does the maths. AI only does the talking.

Every number has a source

Load, fitness, fatigue, zones and the safe ceiling come from established, referenceable sports-science models. Tap any number to see where it came from and how sure olto is.

On-device AI narrates

Apple's on-device model turns those numbers into plain sentences. It reads; it never calculates. It runs on your iPhone, and Apple's Private Cloud Compute is used only if you switch it on.

Honest when it's guessing

An estimate is labelled as one. A labelled good guess beats a precise-looking wrong number.

Every sport counts

Cycling, running, walking, hiking, rowing, strength, yoga and more, each with its own load, plans and review.

Your data stays yours

It lives on your iPhone and in the services you choose to connect. Nothing is sent anywhere before you've seen it.
